# Predicted Results

This study asks whether demographic characteristics relate to adults' stated trust in AI advice on an everyday classification task, whether those differences are specific to AI, and whether trust tracks the AI's stated accuracy. Data will come from Dataset 1 (Vodrahalli et al., 2022).

## Sample

The analytic samples are expected to be about 500 participants who received AI advice (RQ1), about 1,000 who received AI or human advice (RQ2), and about 170 US art-task participants who received AI advice (RQ3). Stated trust (0–100) is expected to be moderately high on average, with no strong floor or ceiling effect.

## RQ1: Do demographics predict trust in AI advice?

- *How:* bivariate tests (t-tests, ANOVAs, correlations) for each of the six demographics, then one OLS regression with all six plus task, stated accuracy and region as controls; Holm correction across the demographics.

We expect everyday AI use to be the strongest predictor: people who use AI more often should report more trust in AI advice. Age, gender, education, socioeconomic status and programming experience are expected to have small effects, if any.

## RQ2: Are these differences specific to AI?

- *How:* OLS regression on the AI and human conditions combined, testing an advice source × demographic interaction for each of the six demographics; Holm correction across the interactions.

Following the original study, we expect AI advice to be trusted at least as much as human advice. If the demographic differences in RQ1 reflect attitudes toward AI specifically, they should be stronger for AI advice than for human advice (significant interactions). If they reflect a general tendency to trust advice, they should appear in both conditions (no interactions).

## RQ3: Does trust track the AI's stated accuracy?

- *How:* one-way ANOVA comparing trust across the three stated accuracy levels (65%, 80%, 95%), with a linear trend contrast.

We expect trust to rise as the AI's stated accuracy increases, with the lowest trust at 65% and the highest at 95%. We also expect the size of this increase to differ little across demographic groups.

## Summary

| Research question | Predicted finding |
|---|---|
| RQ1: Demographics → trust in AI | Everyday AI use predicts higher trust; other demographics small or null |
| RQ2: Specific to AI? | AI advice trusted at least as much as human advice; interactions test whether RQ1 effects are AI-specific |
| RQ3: Tracks stated accuracy? | Trust increases from 65% to 80% to 95% |
